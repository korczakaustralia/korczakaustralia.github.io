World Children’s Day 2026:

World Children's Day is on the 20 November each year. This year is the 37th anniversary of the adoption of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child – a special moment where governments around the world (including Australia) agreed on 54 different rights that every child under 18 years old should have to live a safe, healthy and happy life.

The Convention on the Rights of the Child is based on what a child needs to grow, learn and live a safe and healthy life with dignity and respect. It is for every child, regardless of gender, religion, culture or ethnicity.  Is this so for even almost every child in Australia?

On World Children’s Day, we’re standing up for every child’s right to grow up healthy in body and mind. Every child has the right to feel and be healthy – not just physically, but mentally and emotionally and in every part of who they are.

A healthy childhood starts with the basics: nutritious food, clean water, healthcare, safety and support. But it also means so much more: love, connection and the chance to feel safe, heard and cared for. When children have all of this, they don’t just survive, they grow, dream and thrive.

Australia is not at war, yet too many children in our country do not have their basic needs met nor do they have the safety, support and  respect for who they are, in many cases, enough for them to survive, let alone thrive.  

For many years Australia was known as the Lucky Country. Now children are not only victims of terror, they are attacking others of all ages, school refusal, youth depression, suicide, and  acts of religious and racial discrimination are increasing rapidly and religious discrimination is higher than ever before?     Why?  

In Korczak’s words,  “ One should not leave the world as it is.”  

Yes, it is difficult for one person to make a difference, but think about when someone helped you when you were in trouble. Did it make a difference? Even small acts of kindness can make a start. Standing up for what you believe in makes sure other people know  who you are and why you do what you do. Understanding the other not only helps them, it helps you too. To everyone else other than within your neighbourhood or community, you are the other!

“This World Children’s Day, let us listen to children. And let us amplify their voices as they stand up for their rights”  UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres.

Activities Page

Children’s voices in every classroom

On Thursday 20 November the Janusz Korczak Association Australia will mark World Children’s Day, the anniversary of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child. This year we are inviting every school on the Mornington Peninsula to hand the day over to its students.

Korczak believed that children are not the people of tomorrow but the people of today. World Children’s Day is the one day in the year when that idea is on the world’s calendar, and we want it heard in classrooms, staff rooms and school councils across Australia.

How schools can take part

  • Student-led assembly. Students choose the theme, write the script and run the morning assembly, with teachers in the audience.
  • Rights in our words. Each class rewrites one article of the Convention in language that makes sense to them and displays it at the school gate.
  • A question for the principal. Every student writes one question about how decisions are made at their school. The principal answers a selection of them in front of the whole school.

Schools that register will receive a short activity pack and a certificate of participation for every student. Register by emailing info@korczakaustralia.com.au with your school name and the number of students taking part.

Save the date

When: Thursday 20 November 2026, any time during the school day

Where: your own school; an online gathering of participating schools will be held at 2.00 pm AEDT

Cost: free

More about the day: UNICEF World Children’s Day and our own Children’s Rights page.

Let’s get loud to make Australia a better place for every person under the age of 18!

We believe that every child should have the chance to speak up about issues they care about. It’s their future, but too often their voices aren’t heard.  

This World Children’s Day, we invite every child in Australia to raise their voices for something that matters: every child’s right to be healthy and happy.

 Article 24 of the Convention of the Rights of the Child: Every child has the right to be healthy. 

This means having good healthcare, support for their mental health, clean water, nutritious food, a safe place to live, and information that helps them stay well and safe.

Yet, around the world, many children are denied this right due to conflict, disaster, displacement and inequality. And here in Australia, rising inequality means that even in our classrooms, some children are missing out on more than just the basics.

This World Children's Day your classroom can help students explore what it means to be healthy and happy, and why every child, everywhere, deserves that chance.
